Overview

This course will cover the basics of how to critically ideate, design and create graphics and user interfaces using software and coding languages. We will cover the history, theory and implementation of graphics and user interfaces from the beginning to contemporary computing to present day. Students will first learn about graphics in OpenGL, image processing, and 3D pipelines. Next students will learn about standard web based GUIs, UX design principles, games, and Human Interface Devices as ways of understanding interfaces. Finally students will create their own application for generating graphics using a custom build interface of their choosing.

Learning Objectives

The goal for this class is primarily to develop a conceptual and problem solving based approach to coding graphics and how we design interfaces. By the end of the term you should understand the intent behind your choices in design and aesthetics and understand the historical lineage they come out of or break. Secondary to these aims, I want you to feel more comfortable programming in general but specifically for web based interfaces and graphics. Finally, I want you to develop a critical understanding of the world you interact with and the work you make within it.

Grading

Grades are determined through a combination of participation, small assignments, and a large cumulative assignment at the end of the course. Eight smaller assignments each comprise 5% of your grade, your final assignment comprises 36% of your grade, and participation and attendance comprise the final 24% of your grade.

Technicals

Technicals are ungraded readings, tutorials, and videos which should be completed before the class they are listed under (excepting the first week where technicals will be completed in class). Your ability to participate in class will be hindered if you do not complete the technicals but their completion earns no points.

Small Assignments

Small assignments are graded on a scale of 5 points. All assignments can be improved upon after completion for more points.

  • 3 points are awarded for an assignment which fulfills the basic requirements of the assignment and is submitted on time.
  • 4 points are awarded for assignments which show originality and careful thought behind the work. This means: comments are coherent, code functions as intended, and the product of the code shows style and innovation within the constraints and technical limitations of a beginner course
  • 5 points are awarded to assignments which go above and beyond. This should be something that surprises and impresses your peers.

Final Assignment

Your final assignment will be graded on 4 different categories between 1-9. This will be discussed in more detail when we discuss the final assignment in week 8.

  • Technical Proficiency
  • Usability
  • Graphical Output
  • Ingenuity and Style

Participation

Participation in this course means active engagement in discussion, assignment shares, and the community of the class. Attendance in this class is mandatory. Each class is graded on a scale of 2 points. If you are here on time and engaged, you will receive 2 points. If you are here, but not participating, you will receive 1 point. Unless you are ill, if you are absent you will receive 0 points unless you have communicated with me in advance of class.

Week 10 - 12: Final Project

We will have short lectures but class time will mostly be spent working, problem solving with your peers, and sharing intermediate progress.

Assignment:

Design a program that is a tool for making screen space graphical objects (images, videos, 3d models). The tool does not need to be linear in its function. The tool should not be deterministic. If two users with different stylistic desires approach the program they should be able to make things that look like they are made by two different people. The tool should produce interesting and novel output.

You must design an interface for the tool. The tool should be easy to use. If it is not easy to use, it should be fun enough to use that you want to use it anyway. Think of games as a way of generating this friction into productive modes. This interface can be whatever you like, fully digital, HID controlled, sound or video input based, you can make your own HID that measures perspiration, whatever works works.

Unless you are making specific hardware, your tool needs to be able to reasonably run on a macbook and a PC laptop. If you are using specific hardware you need to make it reasonably connectable to a macbook and a PC laptop. It can be a web app, it can be a desktop program, it can be malware, whatever you like.
